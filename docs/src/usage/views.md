# Views

Every view is a dockable tile. The default layout puts three tab groups
side by side — _shape_ (Waveforms, Templates, Probe, Summary),
_comparison_ (CCG, Features, Amplitudes, ISI, Rate, Similar) and
_assessment_ (Quality, Suggest, Stats, Raster, Drift, Contam·time); PSTH sits in the comparison group — above
a full-width Trace, so a waveform overlay, a correlogram grid and a feature
scatter of the same selection are visible at once, as in phy.

- Drag a tab title to move it into another group, or to the edge of a
  tile to split it. Drag the borders to resize.
- `×` on a tab closes the view; the `+` at the right of a tab bar or the
  **View** menu in the top bar reopens it. **View → Reset layout** restores
  the default.
- Every view can be maximized or popped out: the two buttons at the right of
  a tab bar act on its active tab. Maximize fills the view area with that one
  view (header **Restore** button, `Esc` or `Ctrl+Shift+M` brings the layout
  back); pop-out moves the view to its own native window, leaving "In its own
  window" and a **Bring back** button in its tile, and closing that window
  brings the view back. `F11` toggles full screen in either window. The
  `:max` and `:pop` snippets do the same for the view under the pointer. A
  popped-out window handles the same keyboard shortcuts while it has focus
  (`K` splits, `Esc` clears a lasso and never closes it).
- The layout, table sort/filter, trace settings and last selected cluster
  are saved per dataset in `<dataset>/.sorrel/ui.json` and restored on the
  next open.

The cluster table stays in the left panel. Clusters in the selection are
coloured by their position in it (first = blue, second = orange, …), and
every view uses the same colour for the same cluster.

**Cross-talk** (Sorrel extra). The views share four kinds of state, so a pick in one shows in all the others:

- **Spike brush.** One set of spikes at a time, and the last writer wins. It comes from a Features lasso or k-means part, an Amplitudes lasso or threshold, a Spike data or Tmpl features lasso, or the ISI refractory band. Every spike view draws the brushed spikes in brush ink: white in dark, near-black in light. With a lasso or k-means brush, ISI, Waveforms and Quality also preview the split: the inside part dashed, the outside part dotted, and "if split" rows with the change in composite score.
- **Time range.** Shift-drag along a time axis (Amplitudes, Rate, Drift, Raster, Features' time cells). Spikes outside it fade, or hide with **only in range**.
- **Hovered spike.** The spike under the pointer in Features, Amplitudes or Spike data, or the one clicked in Trace. It is ringed in the point views, marked with a line in the time views, and drawn bold in Waveforms. Alt-click seeks Trace to it.
- **Ghosts.** Hovering a Similar or Suggest row shows that cluster as a grey ghost (points, dashed mean, CCG row, 3D hull) with the merge's quality change. Hovering a History row shows the clusters as they were before that edit, as dashed hulls in Features 3D.

The brush and the time range appear as chips in the top bar. Click a chip's × or press Esc to clear them. A curation edit drops the brush, because its spike indices no longer mean the same spikes.

**Features 3D** (Sorrel extra; the **3D** toggle in the Features header) shows
the same points in three dimensions of the channel set (time, then PC1 and
PC2 on every channel: 17 dimensions and 680 triples for eight channels)
behind an orbit camera. It is for inspection only: lasso, k-means and split
stay in the 2D grid. Every triple of the set is ranked by how well it
separates the selected clusters (mean simplified silhouette on at most 5000
points, every axis scaled to 0–1); with one cluster selected the k-means parts
are the labels, and with no parts the total variance. A wide pane lists the
top 20 in a sidebar with a score bar and a **show all N** toggle that expands
the list into a scroll area; a narrow one offers the top 20 in a combo box.
Your pick is remembered as its three axes, so it survives re-ranking, and
gives way to the best triple when the channel set changes and one of its axes
is gone. The focused 2D plot is **pinned** when you enter 3D (a
"pinned · x × y ×" chip under the toolbar, following the focused cell): only
the triples holding both its axes are listed, as "+ third axis" rows, and the
best of them is the default. **×** on the chip lists every triple; the "pin x
× y" button pins again. The channels of the triple on show are highlighted in
Waveforms (with the grid beside the scene, in the Expansive layout, the
focused 2D cell's channels win). Each selected cluster gets a convex hull of the 95% of its
points nearest the median: translucent and filled for sorter-made (`Auto`)
units, wireframe for Sorrel-made ones. A cluster made by a split also shows
its parent before the split as a dashed grey hull (the union of the siblings
the split produced; merged units show only their current hull). A closed
lasso is extruded along the third axis as a prism when its two dimensions are
both in the triple, otherwise a note says which triple shows it. The k-means
part on show gets a dotted hull in the link colour, and its points turn white
(dark theme) or black (light). The legend names every cluster with its origin
(`Auto[kilosort4]`, `Sorrel[Manual]`, …) and each overlay. Drag orbits, Shift-drag (or
middle-drag) pans, the wheel zooms and double-click resets the camera.

Cross-talk in 3D: the app-wide **spike brush** (a Features lasso, k-means part,
ISI violators, …) draws its spikes in brush ink (white in dark, near-black in
light), a little bigger and on top; the **time brush** fades the spikes outside
its range, or hides them with "only in range". The pointer picks the nearest
spike within 6 px, which rings it in the accent colour here and in every other
spike view (and the ring follows a spike hovered elsewhere). Hovering a
Similar or Suggest row draws that cluster as a grey dashed hull ("ghost cN");
hovering a History row draws each cluster the edit changed as it was before it
(dashed, "before #n", restricted to the spikes shown). The Features colour mode
(time, depth) colours the points the same way as the 2D grid. With spike depths
available (the Drift map's source) the **space** family joins the axes: `depth`
takes part in the ranked triples like any PC (spike x is not read yet).

The Trace toolbar offers **per shank** CMR when the probe has more than one
shank (`channel_shanks.npy`), referencing each shank by its own median.

Plots follow the egui theme, so light mode renders light plot backgrounds.
